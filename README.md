<p> Eighty-eight percent. </p> <br>

<p> That's the percentage of individuals that struggle to find, understand and use everyday health information in the United States. According to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, roughly 80 million people have low or limited health literacy. </p> <br>

<p> Health literacy is essential. It is a person’s ability to access, understand and apply information to make informed healthcare decisions. Strong health literacy helps bridge the gap between the public’s understanding and how healthcare systems operate. Yet, this ongoing literacy crisis also makes it difficult for patients to interact and engage in conversations with their healthcare providers. Many individuals either do not know what health information is important to know, how to access that information, or what measures to take when presented with their health information. Communities in states like New York also lack access to basic social infrastructures, limiting the number of patients that can get quality care. With all of these factors, insufficient health literacy levels translate to an additional one million hospital visits. Ultimately, the gap between a patient’s understanding and a prescriber’s directions becomes a factor in a patient’s compliance and literacy. </p> <br>

<p> Last month, 11 months after founding and creating MedFacts 101, I decided to target one important aspect of health literacy: the ability to read and understand a prescription label. While for some individuals instructions may seem clear, many others find it extremely difficult to understand. </p> <br>

<p> Studies have shown that about half (46%) of patients misunderstand at least one dosing instruction. This issue can be sourced to many factors, including information clutter, vague dosage phrasing, tiny fonts, and medical jargon. Fortunately, there have been efforts to combat this, such as the U.S. Pharmacopeia’s strict guidelines and efforts for “patient-centric label designs.” </p> <br>

<p> To help solve this, I created Litsalus, a Duolingo-inspired interactive educational game that teaches players how to safely and accurately read prescription labels. </p> <br>

<p> Built using AI design and development tools, the game offers two distinct pathways: Regular Mode for clear, direct instructions, and Advanced Mode for mastering complex medical abbreviations. In the coming months, I hope to introduce lessons modeled after my Instagram post “Decoding a Prescription Label"
